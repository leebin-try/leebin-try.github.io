We read a great deal each day, but rarely stop to ask where the information goes. Some becomes a bookmark or screenshot; some disappears in minutes. A growing collection does not necessarily leave behind more understanding. A small digital garden is one way to keep the thinking.

## Give an idea somewhere to land

A digital garden need not be a complicated knowledge-management system. A few connected articles, unfinished notes and a revisable personal site can be enough.

> A useful record does not preserve everything. It gives important ideas a chance to develop.

Not every corner needs to be tidy. An unresolved question or an experiment with doubts still deserves a place.

## Start with the smallest useful structure

The first version needs only three parts:

1. **Articles** for developed arguments.
2. **Notes** for shorter, changing ideas.
3. **About** for what the space cares about and what is being built.

Keep the technology similarly direct: Markdown holds content, Astro makes pages and Git records changes. Establish that loop before adding search, tags and subscriptions.

```text
A question -> a practical attempt -> a record -> the next connection
```

## Write for your future self

One useful reader is yourself a few months later. Recording the context, failed attempts and trade-offs of debugging is more useful than saving only the final command.

An article need not be finished in one sitting. Revise it as understanding develops, rather than freezing the thinking at publication.

## Steady work can become a landscape

A personal site’s value need not be measured by traffic. It can come from a sustainable rhythm: write when an idea appears, revise when it becomes clearer, and share what is worth keeping.

Leave room for one small thought today. Its eventual shape can take time.
