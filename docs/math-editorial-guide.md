# Math editorial guide

Math has a dedicated hub at `/math/` and `/zh/math/`. Articles use the shared reading layout, paired Markdown, sticky contents and the site's design standard. The first article is a mathematical reading guide to the result announced on November 20, 2026; its research receipt is [here](math/riemann-2026-research.md).

## Editorial requirements

- State the exact problem, hypotheses, quantified objects, exceptions and conclusion. Define notation before using it, including normalization and parameter dependence.
- Check a proof announcement against primary sources before describing its status. Separate the author's claim, statement matching, axiom audit, kernel verification, mathematical exposition, and institutional recognition. Date the status and attribute checks to whoever ran them.
- Explain the mechanism, not just a list of sophisticated subjects. Show at least one substantial derivation, the role of every major input, and where the difficult inputs remain.
- Identify the version being explained. An original archive, a shortened rewriting and a human-readable draft are distinct artifacts. Pin code references when available; do not suggest that a rewriting is a line-by-line explanation of its predecessor.
- Say what was source-read, calculated or executed for the article. Never turn a third-party verification into a claim of a local independent replay.
- Mark toy examples, schematic diagrams and finite numerical illustrations. Explain how they differ from the theorem's objects. Numerical agreement on a finite range does not establish an asymptotic bound.
- Link primary sources near version-specific claims. Provide a reading order, concepts to learn, official course links, exercises and concrete learning deliverables. Do not promise that a short course plan suffices to verify a research-level proof.
- Keep conclusions explicit and explain what evidence or future work would change them. Article length is not a proxy for correctness or depth.

## Content and rendering

Add the Chinese article under `src/content/posts/` and its matching English edition under `src/content/posts/en/`. Use `category: Math`, `sample: false`, and the `math` record required by the content schema. Reading-guide metadata records the original source, verification source, evidence date and pinned proof commit. If a future Math genre needs different metadata, extend the discriminated policy deliberately rather than inventing a proof commit.

Use `$...$` for inline mathematics and a separate `$$` block for display mathematics. Astro renders these at build time with remark-math and rehype-katex, with local KaTeX CSS/fonts and MathML. No remote equation service or browser-side math execution is needed. Long displays scroll locally; the page must not overflow.

Preserve the same heading depth/order, formulas, figure paths, source version and numerical facts across languages. Use meaningful alternative text and captions in each language. Diagrams shared across editions use English labels with translated explanations in the article. Provide full-size links; do not crop axes or labels.

The Riemann guide's original vector figures and finite prime-power CSV are generated by `scripts/build-math-assets.py`; `--check` uses only the standard library to verify reproducibility and the sharing image. Run `node scripts/render-math-cover.mjs` after changing the cover SVG generator. A schematic's points are never presented as computed zeros.

## Checks

Run `npm run check`. The design contract includes the Math hub and long mathematical article in both languages at every representative width. Behavioral tests check navigation, archive filtering, formula rendering, MathML, source version, table of contents, language mapping and raw-data access. Content checks preserve paired Math metadata and numerical examples. The existing figure/link audit checks all built internal links; the Math generator separately checks its new assets.

Review relevant screenshots on desktop and mobile. Automated checks can reject broken formulas or horizontal overflow, but cannot establish that a mathematical argument is valid or an explanation adequate.
